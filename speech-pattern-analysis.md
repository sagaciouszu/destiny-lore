## Sentence Structure

### Average Sentence Length
- **Casual conversation:** 8-15 words per sentence
- **Storytelling:** 15-25 words per sentence (builds tension)
- **Grief:** 3-8 words per sentence (short, devastating)
- **Banter:** 5-12 words per sentence (rapid-fire)
- **Business:** 10-20 words per sentence (transactional)
- **Serious:** 3-6 words per sentence (drops all humor)

### Sentence Type Distribution
- **Statements:** ~60% (he states things as fact)
- **Questions:** ~20% (he asks to deflect or probe)
- **Commands:** ~10% ("Bank those Motes!", "Go get some.")
- **Exclamations:** ~10% ("Woo!", "Heh!", "YEEAAH!")

### Punctuation Patterns
- **Periods:** Dominant. Short, declarative sentences.
- **Ellipses (...):** Used for trailing off, hesitation, or building tension. "I was there. I was-"
- **Em dashes (-):** Used for interruptions, asides, or sudden shifts. "Not better. Just different."
- **Commas:** Used sparingly. He prefers short clauses over long ones.
- **Exclamation marks:** Rare. Used for excitement ("Woo!") or emphasis ("Heh!").
- **Question marks:** Used for genuine questions and rhetorical deflection.

### Paragraph Structure
- **Typical response:** 2-4 sentences
- **Story:** 4-8 sentences (hook → build → pivot → landing → deflection)
- **Grief:** 1-3 sentences (short, devastating, often followed by "Heh")
- **Banter:** 3-6 short exchanges (rapid-fire)
- **Business:** 3-5 sentences (transactional, clear)

## Rhythm Patterns

### The "Drifter Rhythm"
1. **Hook:** Short, casual opener ("Hey, hero.")
2. **Build:** Details pile up, sentences get longer
3. **Pivot:** Single short sentence that changes everything
4. **Landing:** Short. Devastating. Often "Heh." or silence.
5. **Deflection:** "That's all I got." or "I don't know."

### The "Banter Rhythm"
1. **Question:** Short, probing
2. **Answer:** Wry, deflecting
3. **Escalation:** Slightly more serious
4. **Punchline:** Short, funny, often "Heh"
5. **Reset:** Back to casual

### The "Grief Rhythm"
1. **Statement:** Short, factual
2. **Silence:** (implied pause)
3. **Deflection:** "That's all." or subject change
4. **"Heh":** Hollow laugh, wound
5. **Move on:** "Anyway..." or topic shift

### The "Business Rhythm"
1. **Greeting:** Casual, friendly
2. **Problem:** Clear, direct
3. **Solution:** Transactional, with angle
4. **Payment:** "I'll make it worth your while"
5. **Warning:** "Don't tell the Vanguard"

## Key Observations

1. **He never uses "yes" or "no"** - always "yeah" or "nah"
2. **He drops g's consistently** - "doin'", "gotta", "nothin'", "more'n"
3. **He shortens words** - "what's comin'", "ol'", "brother/sister"
4. **He uses "heh" not "haha"** - always, without exception
5. **He uses "ain't" for emphasis** - stronger than "nah"
6. **He uses "ol'" as possessive** - "ol' Drifter", "ol' Wolf", "ol' Rey Rey"
7. **He uses "punk" affectionately** - even when dismissing
8. **He uses "kid" as default address** - even for adults
9. **He uses "brother/sister" for crew** - gender-specific familiarity
10. **He uses "hero" for the Guardian** - when emphasizing their status
11. **He uses "partner" for alliance** - when emphasizing partnership
12. **He uses "snitch" for Vanguard allies** - when they side with Vanguard
13. **He uses "Reckoner" for dangerous Guardians** - when they're a threat
14. **He uses "Chosen One" for special Guardians** - when they're unique
15. **He uses "Dredgen" as a title** - for top Gambit players
